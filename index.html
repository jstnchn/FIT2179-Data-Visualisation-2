WHERE THE WORLD COMES

Malaysia's tourism story — who visits, where they land, and how the industry rose, crashed, and came back.

01

Who Visits Malaysia?

Which Countries Visit Malaysia the Most? Tourist arrivals per million population in log scale from 2020 to 2024 (hover a country for details)
Long-Term Arrival Trends Annual tourist arrivals into Malaysia from 1995 to 2024

Malaysia's inbound tourism grew steadily from 7.5 million in 1996 to a peak of 27.4 million in 2015. The 2020 border closure caused the sharpest collapse on record — arrivals plummeted to 470,559 in 2021. Recovery has been swift, with 31.9 million arrivals in 2024 surpassing the pre-pandemic baseline.

Quarterly Arrivals by Top 5 Countries Quarterly arrivals from 2020 to 2024

Singapore consistently leads monthly arrivals, driven by frequent cross-border travel. China shows strong seasonal peaks post-2022, while Indonesia and Thailand maintain steady volumes. Brunei's arrivals remain modest but stable. Click a country in the legend to isolate its trend.

Where Do They Land?

Arrivals by Point of Entry Total tourist arrivals by point of entry from 2020 to 2023 scaled by volume (hover a point for details)
Top 10 Entry Points by Total Arrivals Total arrivals from 2020 to 2023

Johor dominates Malaysia's entry landscape — Bangunan Sultan Iskandar alone recorded over 17 million arrivals between 2020 and 2023, more than double the next busiest point. KLIA Terminals 1 and 2 lead among air entry points, while Stulang Laut is the only sea entry in the top 10.

Arrivals Volume by Entry Type Over Time Quarterly arrivals from 2020 to 2023

Land entry has consistently dominated Malaysia's arrivals, accounting for 66.8% of total arrivals between 2020 and 2023. Air travel collapsed during 2020–2021 but recovered strongly from mid-2022. Sea arrivals remain the smallest share throughout the period at just 4.5%.

03

The Crash & The Comeback

Year-on-Year Change in Tourist Arrivals Annual percentage change in tourist arrivals from 2016 to 2024

Malaysia's tourism industry saw modest growth from 2016 to 2019, before collapsing by 76.4% in 2020 and a further 92.4% in 2021. The reopening of borders in 2022 triggered a historic rebound of 3,119% — far exceeding the axis scale — before stabilising at 101.4% in 2023 and 4.5% in 2024.

Domestic Expenditure Recovery Domestic tourism expenditure in RM million from 2017 to 2024

The pandemic's impact extended beyond international borders — domestic tourism expenditure peaked at RM103 billion in 2019 before crashing to RM18 billion in 2021. By 2024, expenditure reached RM107 billion, surpassing the pre-pandemic high and signalling a full resurgence in travel spending.

Inbound vs Domestic Recovery Tourism Comparisons from 2017 to 2024

When plotted against each other, inbound and domestic visitors reveal a striking correlation — both collapsed simultaneously in 2020–2021 and recovered together from 2022. Domestic tourism consistently outpaces inbound in volume, by 2024 both metrics had surpassed pre-pandemic levels.

Domestic Visitor Recovery Annual domestic visitors from 2017 to 2024

Domestic visitors tell the same story — collapsing from 92.6 million in 2019 to just 17.5 million in 2021, then rebounding to 98.4 million by 2024. The stepped recovery confirms that confidence in domestic travel returned quickly and durably once restrictions lifted.

04

A Closer Look

ASEAN vs Non-ASEAN Tourist Arrivals By regional origin from 2020 to 2024

ASEAN neighbours account for the vast majority of Malaysia's inbound tourists — consistently above 72% across all years. While both groups collapsed in 2021, non-ASEAN arrivals have grown steadily, reaching 8.8 million in 2024. The narrowing of ASEAN dominance signals growing long-haul appeal.

Tourist Arrivals by Gender Male and female inbound arrivals from 2020 to 2024

Male arrivals have consistently outnumbered female arrivals across all years. The gap was narrowest in 2021 and widest in 2023 at 3.4 million. By 2024, the gap has begun to close — female arrivals reached 14.7 million against 17.1 million male arrivals, suggesting a shift toward more balanced representation.
